# NeuroBridge EEG Lab design context

_Status: web-first product form confirmed; visual direction remains provisional._

---

## Design Context

### Users

The primary user is an EEG beginner who understands scientific questions but is not yet fluent in signal processing or MNE-Python. They learn through deliberate experimentation: changing one parameter, predicting what will happen, inspecting synchronized views, revealing simulation truth, and explaining the discrepancy. Secondary users are instructors and researchers who want a reproducible demonstration environment rather than a clinical analysis system.

### Brand Personality

Calm, exploratory, and scientifically honest. The product should feel like a well-made laboratory instrument joined to an annotated textbook: precise enough to trust, approachable enough to touch, and explicit about uncertainty.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light-first, instrument-like workspace with a restrained dark inspection mode. Favor an asymmetric three-region layout: controls and lesson context on the left, the primary signal or network canvas in the center, and interpretation plus provenance on the right. Avoid generic dashboard card grids, neon-on-dark styling, decorative gradients, and visualizations that hide units or uncertainty.

### Design Principles

1. Show cause and effect in the same viewport.
2. Keep units, reference, sampling rate, data rank, and analysis space visible.
3. Reveal complexity progressively without concealing scientific assumptions.
4. Pair every result with an explanation, caveat, and route back to the generating parameters.
5. Never imply that real EEG has a known truth or that functional connectivity proves causality.
6. Make every run reproducible from a visible seed, versioned recipe, and exportable record.
7. Let beginners stay in a guided path while progressively revealing the same controls used by advanced analysis modules.
8. Keep browser and future desktop experiences visually and behaviorally equivalent.
